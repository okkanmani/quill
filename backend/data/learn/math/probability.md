## What is probability?

**Probability** is a number that describes **how likely** something is. When every outcome starts **fairly** (same chance), a useful Grade 5 form is:

**Probability (event) ≈ favourable outcomes ÷ equally likely outcomes in total.**

**Example:** One fair roll of a 1–6 die gives six evenly split outcomes. The chance you roll exactly a **5** is **one** out of **six**, written **1/6**.

## Between zero and one

Probabilities belong on a scale where:

- **0** means **impossible** (given the assumptions you wrote down).
- **1** means **certain**.
- Fractions/decimals strictly **between** 0 and 1 mean “might happen”—sometimes easy, sometimes hard.

Percents bigger than **100%** describe something else (“growth”), not a valid probability sitting at a single uncertain event.

## Language for likelihood (Ontario-style words)

Rough guide—words are softer than fractions:

| Phrase | Typical feel |
|--------|----------------|
| impossible | sticks at **zero** chance |
| unlikely | closer to **zero** than to **one half** |
| as likely / even chance | about **half** |
| likely | closer to **one** than to **half** |
| certain | **one** |

Calibrating those words grows with lived experience—you still check the numbers when someone gives them!

## Opposite (“not”) — complement shortcut

Sometimes you want **P(not A)** — the probability **A does not happen**. When outcomes split cleanly:

**P(not A) = 1 − P(A)**

(With fractions that means “the whole takeaway the part”.)

Rain vs no-rain forecasts are everyday examples—you still notice if the wording matches the numbers.

## List outcomes patiently

Fair **two coins** flipping once each (order matters: first coin vs second).

Outcomes arranged:

**HH, HT, TH, TT** — four tidy and **equally likely** setups if coins don’t cheat.

So **P(HH)** = **1/4**, **P(exactly one head)** = **2/4 = 1/2**.

Skipping one row in tables is probably the sneakiest textbook mistake!

## Spinner fairness

Spinner probability follows **fraction of identical wedges** **only** when each wedge grabs the wheel equally (same central angle/size). Pretty colours don’t create fairness—the geometry decides.

Unequal wedges? Outcomes weren’t symmetric to begin with—you handle them by explicit areas or wording in the worksheet.

## Long-run intuition (friendly “lots of repeats” idea)

Repeating fairly **lots** keeps the **fraction of hits** creeping toward theoretical probability—even though streaks (“three tails”) still arise by luck alone.

Scientists call tightening pattern **law of large numbers** deeper on the road—you only need intuition now.

## Fairness in games & models

Discuss “fair”: symmetric rules splitting winning chances cleanly; **biased** if someone engineered hidden advantages.

Thinking about probability stops us narrating coincidence as destiny—“hot hand” myths appear everywhere.
