**Algebra** uses **letters** (variables) to stand for numbers. This lets us write **rules**, describe **patterns**, and find **unknown** values.

## Matchstick patterns

Each **L** uses 2 matchsticks.

| Number of Ls (n) | Matchsticks |
|------------------|-------------|
| 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 4 |
| 3 | 6 |
| 7 | 14 |

**Rule:** matchsticks = **2 × n** (or **2n**)

The letter **n** can be any counting number we choose.

## Variables and expressions

- **Variable:** a letter for an unknown or changing number (n, x, y)
- **Algebraic expression:** numbers and variables with operations — **2n**, **3x + 5**, **y − 7**

**Expression vs equation:**
- **2n + 1** is an expression (no equals sign)
- **2n + 1 = 11** is an equation (states two things are equal)

## Equations and solutions

An **equation** asks: what value of the variable makes it true?

**n + 5 = 12** → n = **7** (check: 7 + 5 = 12 ✓)

**2m = 14** → m = **7**

The value that makes the equation true is the **solution**.

## Using inverse operations

| To undo | Do the opposite |
|---------|-----------------|
| + 5 | − 5 |
| − 3 | + 3 |
| × 4 | ÷ 4 |
| ÷ 2 | × 2 |

**x + 8 = 15** → x = 15 − 8 = **7**  
**y − 3 = 10** → y = 10 + 3 = **13**  
**3p = 21** → p = 21 ÷ 3 = **7**

## Practical rules

Convert stories into algebra:

- "A number plus 10 is 25" → **n + 10 = 25**
- "After buying a pen for ₹15, I have ₹35 left" → **x − 15 = 35**

Always **check** your answer by substituting back into the original equation.
