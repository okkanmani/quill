We often compare two quantities — sometimes by **difference**, sometimes by **division**. When comparison by "how many times" is useful, we use **ratio**. When two ratios are equal, we have **proportion**.

## Ratio

A **ratio** compares two quantities of the **same kind** by division.

Written as **a : b** or **a/b** (b ≠ 0).

**Father's weight 75 kg, Isha's weight 25 kg:**
- Ratio of father to Isha = 75 : 25 = **3 : 1** (father is 3 times as heavy)

**Pen ₹10, pencil ₹2:**
- Ratio of pen to pencil = 10 : 2 = **5 : 1**

## Order matters

Ratio of **girls to boys** is not the same as **boys to girls** unless the counts are equal.

In a class of 20 boys and 40 girls:
- Girls : total = 40 : 60 = **2 : 3**
- Boys : total = 20 : 60 = **1 : 3**

## Equivalent ratios

Multiply or divide **both** parts by the same number:

**2 : 3 = 4 : 6 = 6 : 9**

Like equivalent fractions, ratios can be simplified to **lowest terms**.

## Proportion

Four numbers **a, b, c, d** are in proportion if:

**a : b = c : d**  (or **a/b = c/d**)

**Example:** 2 : 3 = 4 : 6 ✓ because 2/3 = 4/6

If **a : b = c : d**, then **a × d = b × c** (cross multiplication).

## Unitary method

Find the value of **one unit**, then scale up.

**5 books cost ₹150 → 1 book = ₹30 → 8 books = ₹240**

Steps:
1. Find cost of **one** book
2. Multiply by the number needed

## When to use ratio vs difference

| Situation | Better tool |
|-----------|-------------|
| "10 cm taller" | Difference |
| "3 times as long" | Ratio |
| Sharing in a fixed ratio | Ratio and proportion |
| Mixing recipes | Unitary method |
