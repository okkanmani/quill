Shapes around us are built from **lines** and **curves**. This chapter compares sizes — lengths of segments, types of angles, and names of common shapes.

## Measuring line segments

A line segment has a fixed **length**. Compare segments by:

1. **Observation** — quick but not always reliable
2. **Tracing** — copy one segment onto another
3. **Ruler and divider** — most accurate for everyday use

On a standard ruler, each centimetre is divided into **10 millimetres** (1 mm = 0.1 cm).

## Angles — right and straight

- A **right angle** = quarter turn = **90°** (corner of a book, set-square)
- A **straight angle** = half turn = **180°** (a straight line)

**Acute** < 90° · **Obtuse** between 90° and 180°

## Measuring angles

Use a **protractor** (semi-circle marked 0° to 180°):

1. Place centre on the vertex
2. Align the base line with one arm
3. Read where the other arm crosses the scale

## Types of triangles (by sides)

| Type | Property |
|------|----------|
| **Scalene** | All three sides different |
| **Isosceles** | Two sides equal |
| **Equilateral** | All three sides equal |

## Types of triangles (by angles)

| Type | Property |
|------|----------|
| **Acute-angled** | All angles < 90° |
| **Right-angled** | One angle = 90° |
| **Obtuse-angled** | One angle > 90° |

## Quadrilaterals

A **quadrilateral** has four sides. Special types:

- **Rectangle** — four right angles; opposite sides equal
- **Square** — four right angles; all sides equal
- **Parallelogram** — opposite sides parallel and equal
- **Rhombus** — all sides equal; opposite sides parallel
- **Trapezium** — one pair of parallel sides

## Three-dimensional shapes

| Shape | Faces | Example |
|-------|-------|---------|
| **Cuboid** | 6 rectangles | Box, book |
| **Cube** | 6 equal squares | Dice |
| **Cylinder** | 2 circles + curved surface | Pipe, can |
| **Cone** | 1 circle + curved surface | Ice-cream cone |
| **Sphere** | Curved surface only | Ball |
