**Whole numbers** are the counting numbers together with **0**:

**0, 1, 2, 3, 4, …**

The set of counting numbers **1, 2, 3, …** is called **natural numbers**. When we include **0**, we get whole numbers.

## Predecessor and successor

- The **successor** of a number is **one more** (the next number).
- The **predecessor** of a number is **one less** (the number before it).

| Number | Predecessor | Successor |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| 19 | 18 | 20 |
| 0 | *(none among whole numbers)* | 1 |

**Note:** **0** has no predecessor in the whole numbers. **1** has no predecessor among *natural* numbers, but its predecessor among whole numbers is **0**.

## The number line

Draw a line, mark **0**, then equal steps to the right for **1, 2, 3, …**

- Moving **right** → larger numbers  
- Moving **left** → smaller numbers  

Whole numbers on the number line are **0 and all points to the right** at whole-number steps.

## Larger whole numbers

Patterns you should know:

| Expression | Result |
|------------|--------|
| 9 + 1 | 10 |
| 99 + 1 | 100 |
| 999 + 1 | 1,000 |
| 9,999 + 1 | 10,000 |
| 99,999 + 1 | **1,00,000** (one **lakh**) |

**Greatest 5-digit number:** 99,999  
**Smallest 6-digit number:** 1,00,000  

## Properties of whole numbers

When you add or multiply whole numbers, the answer is again a whole number (**closure**).

| Property | Addition example | Multiplication example |
|----------|------------------|------------------------|
| **Commutative** | 3 + 5 = 5 + 3 | 4 × 7 = 7 × 4 |
| **Associative** | (2 + 3) + 4 = 2 + (3 + 4) | (2 × 3) × 4 = 2 × (3 × 4) |
| **Identity** | n + **0** = n | n × **1** = n |

**Distributive property** links multiplication and addition:

**7 × (3 + 2) = 7 × 3 + 7 × 2**

Subtraction and division are **not** always closed on whole numbers (e.g. 5 − 8 is not a whole number).
