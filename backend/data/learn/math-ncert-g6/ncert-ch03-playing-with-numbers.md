This chapter is about **factors**, **multiples**, **prime numbers**, and ways to test **divisibility** — the building blocks for **HCF** and **LCM**.

## Factors and multiples

- A **factor** (or **exact divisor**) of a number divides it **with no remainder**.
- A **multiple** of a number is the product of that number and any whole number.

**Example — 6**

6 = 1 × 6 = 2 × 3, so **1, 2, 3, and 6** are factors of 6.

Multiples of 6: **6, 12, 18, 24, …**

Every number is a factor of itself and a multiple of itself.

## Prime and composite numbers

- **Prime:** exactly **two** factors — **1** and the number itself.  
  Examples: **2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, …**
- **Composite:** more than two factors.  
  Examples: **4, 6, 8, 9, 10, …**
- **1** is neither prime nor composite.

**Prime factorisation** — write a number as a product of primes:

**12 = 2 × 2 × 3**  
**18 = 2 × 3 × 3**

## Tests for divisibility

| Divisor | Rule (quick check) |
|---------|-------------------|
| **2** | Last digit is even (0, 2, 4, 6, 8). |
| **3** | Sum of digits is divisible by 3. |
| **4** | Number formed by the **last two digits** is divisible by 4. |
| **5** | Last digit is **0** or **5**. |
| **6** | Divisible by **both 2 and 3**. |
| **8** | Number formed by the **last three digits** is divisible by 8. |
| **9** | Sum of digits is divisible by 9. |
| **10** | Last digit is **0**. |
| **11** | Difference between sum of digits in odd and even places is **0 or a multiple of 11**. |

**Example:** Is **2,394** divisible by 3?  
2 + 3 + 9 + 4 = **18**, and 18 ÷ 3 = 6 → **yes**.

## Common factors and HCF

**Common factors** of two or more numbers are factors shared by all of them.

The **Highest Common Factor (HCF)** is the **largest** of those common factors.

**Example:** HCF of **12** and **18**  
Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12  
Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18  
Common: 1, 2, 3, 6 → **HCF = 6**

Use prime factorisation when numbers are large: take the **lowest power** of each common prime.

## Common multiples and LCM

**Common multiples** are multiples shared by two or more numbers.

The **Lowest Common Multiple (LCM)** is the **smallest** positive common multiple.

**Example:** LCM of **4** and **6**  
Multiples of 4: 4, 8, **12**, 16, …  
Multiples of 6: 6, **12**, 18, …  
**LCM = 12**

For prime factorisations, take each prime at the **highest power** that appears.

## Why HCF and LCM matter

- **HCF** — split things into equal largest groups (e.g. tiling a floor with equal square tiles).  
- **LCM** — find when two repeating events happen together (e.g. two bells ring every 4 and 6 minutes — together every **12** minutes).
