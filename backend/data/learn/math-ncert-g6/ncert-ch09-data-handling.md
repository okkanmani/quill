**Data** is a collection of facts, numbers, or observations gathered to answer a question — attendance records, test scores, cricket scoreboards, or favourite fruits in a class.

## Recording data

A simple **list** works for small groups, but counting by scanning names is slow for large classes.

**Better:** organise responses so you can count quickly — tally marks, tables, or grouped counts.

## Organisation with tally marks

| Fruit | Tally | Count |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Banana | //// //// | 10 |
| Apple | //// / | 6 |
| Orange | //// | 5 |
| Guava | //// | 4 |

Group tallies in **bundles of five** (four vertical + one diagonal) for easy counting.

## Pictographs

Use **symbols** (one picture = one unit, or one picture = many units).

Always include a **key** explaining what each symbol stands for.

## Bar graphs

- **Horizontal or vertical bars** — length or height shows the value
- **Equal width** bars with **equal gaps**
- Label **axes** (what is being measured vs. categories)

Bar graphs make it easy to compare categories at a glance.

## Choosing a representation

| Method | Best for |
|--------|----------|
| Tally table | Collecting raw counts |
| Pictograph | Visual display, small counts |
| Bar graph | Comparing categories clearly |

## Reading and interpreting

When you see a graph or table, ask:

1. What is being measured?
2. Which category is **largest** / **smallest**?
3. How much **more** or **less** is one value than another?

**Example:** If 30 students chose maths and 20 chose science, maths had **10 more** votes.
