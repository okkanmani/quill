**Geometry** comes from Greek *Geometron* — *geo* (Earth) + *metron* (measurement). It began with measuring land, building temples, and describing shapes in art and architecture. We still use these ideas in design, engineering, and everyday objects.

## Point

A **point** marks a **location**. It has no length, width, or thickness — we draw it as a tiny dot and name it with a **capital letter** (A, B, C).

## Line segment

A **line segment** is the straight path between **two end points**. It has a definite length.

- End points of segment AB: **A** and **B**
- Written as **AB** or **BA**

## Line

A **line** extends endlessly in **both** directions through two points. It has **no** end points.

- Through points A and B: line **AB**

## Ray

A **ray** starts at one point and goes on endlessly in **one** direction.

- Ray **AP** starts at A, passes through P, and continues past P.

## Intersecting and parallel lines

- **Intersecting lines** meet at one point.
- **Parallel lines** lie in the same plane and **never meet**, no matter how far they are extended.

## Curves

- **Simple curve:** does not cross itself.
- **Open curve:** end points do not meet.
- **Closed curve:** end points meet (forms a loop).

## Polygons

A **polygon** is a simple closed curve made of **line segments**.

| Name | Sides |
|------|-------|
| Triangle | 3 |
| Quadrilateral | 4 |
| Pentagon | 5 |
| Hexagon | 6 |

## Angles

An **angle** is formed when two rays start from the **same point** (vertex).

- Arms: the two rays
- **Acute:** less than 90°
- **Right:** exactly 90°
- **Obtuse:** more than 90° but less than 180°
- **Straight:** 180° (a straight line)

## Circles

A **circle** is the set of all points at the **same distance** from a fixed point called the **centre**.

- **Radius:** centre to any point on the circle
- **Diameter:** a chord through the centre (twice the radius)
- **Chord:** a line segment joining two points on the circle
- **Arc:** a portion of the circle between two points
