We use **numbers** to count, compare, and describe quantities — from students in a class to distances between cities. This chapter reviews how we read, compare, and work with large numbers in the **Indian number system**.

## Comparing numbers

To find the **greatest** or **smallest** in a list, compare **digit by digit** from left to right (highest place value first).

**Example:** Among 1902, 1920, 9201, 9021, 9210 → **9210** is greatest.

When numbers have different numbers of digits, the one with **more digits** is larger (e.g. 59785 > 750).

## Indian place value

| Period | Places (right to left in each period) |
|--------|--------------------------------------|
| **Ones** | Ones, Tens, Hundreds |
| **Thousands** | Thousands, Ten-thousands |
| **Lakhs** | Lakhs, Ten-lakhs |
| **Crores** | Crores, Ten-crores, … |

**Examples:**
- **9,99,999 + 1 = 10,00,000** (one **ten lakh** — smallest 7-digit number)
- **99,99,999 + 1 = 1,00,00,000** (one **crore** — smallest 8-digit number)

## Reading and writing large numbers

Use **commas** in the Indian system: first comma after hundreds, then every two digits (e.g. **7,53,210**).

**Expanded form:** 5,28,492 = 5 × 1,00,000 + 2 × 10,000 + 8 × 1,000 + 4 × 100 + 9 × 10 + 2

## Estimation

When an **exact** count is not needed, we **estimate** (approximate):

- **Nearest ten:** 17 is closer to 20 than to 10 → round to **20**
- **Nearest hundred:** 410 is closer to 400 → **400**
- **General rule for operations:** round each number first, then add/subtract/multiply

Estimation is useful for budgets, guest counts, and quick mental checks — but not when exact amounts matter (e.g. bank balance).

## Units of measurement

| Unit | Relationship |
|------|--------------|
| 1 cm | 10 mm |
| 1 m | 100 cm |
| 1 km | 1,000 m = 10,00,000 mm |
| 1 kg | 1,000 g |
| 1 g | 1,000 mg |
| 1 ℓ | 1,000 ml |

**Prefixes:** **kilo-** means ×1000; **centi-** means ÷100; **milli-** means ÷1000.

## Using brackets

Brackets show **which part to do first**.

**6 notebooks at ₹10 + 7 notebooks at ₹10:**
- (6 + 7) × 10 = **130** ✓
- 6 + 7 × 10 = 6 + 70 = **76** ✗ (multiplication before addition)

Use brackets when you want to add (or group) before multiplying.
